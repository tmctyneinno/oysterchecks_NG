PART A. UNDERSTAND THE PLATFORM
==========================================================================================

A1. WHAT OYSTERCHECKS IS
------------------------
OysterChecks is one platform for governance, risk, compliance, financial crime and assurance,
for banks, payment companies, remittance firms, digital-asset businesses and other regulated
organisations. Version 1.2 integrates two new capabilities natively:

   - Perimeter Intelligence: global regulatory intelligence. It tracks the laws and rules in
     every jurisdiction where the group operates, spots changes, works out which legal
     entities they apply to, and maps them to obligations, controls and actions.
     (This used to be planned as a separate product, "Morgans Perimeter". It is now part of
     OysterChecks.)
   - Digital Assets Intelligence: crypto and virtual-asset risk: wallet screening, blockchain
     monitoring, tracing and the Travel Rule.

The five layers:
   1. OysterChecks core: identity, legal entities, roles, workflow, evidence, audit, integrations
      and reporting, shared by everything else.
   2. Financial Crime Intelligence: KYC, KYB, beneficial owners, sanctions, PEP, adverse media,
      transaction monitoring, EDD, investigations and SAR or STR reporting.
   3. Enterprise GRC and Assurance: policies, risks, controls, continuous control monitoring,
      incidents, third parties, three-lines assurance and regulatory reporting.
   4. Perimeter Intelligence: jurisdiction packs, regulatory sources, change detection,
      applicability, obligation mapping and cross-border impact.
   5. Digital Assets Intelligence: wallet screening, blockchain monitoring, tracing, Travel
      Rule workflows and crypto investigations.

The problem it solves: risk and compliance teams usually find out something went wrong weeks
later, from spreadsheets. Regulatory changes are tracked in emails. Each country has its own
spreadsheet. Controls are "effective" because someone said so, evidence is scattered, and the
board report is out of date before it is read.

The demo company: "Ajala Financial Group", with five legal entities:
   Ajala Bank UK Ltd (UK, PRA and FCA) . . . . . . . . most detailed records live here
   Ajala Bank Nigeria Plc (Nigeria, CBN, NFIU, NDPC)
   Ajala Remittance LLC (UAE, CBUAE)
   Ajala Digital Assets Ltd (Nigeria, SEC Nigeria)
   Ajala Payments Ireland Ltd (Ireland, EU)


A2. THE BIG IDEA: ONE TRACEABILITY CHAIN
----------------------------------------
Version 1.2 makes this the spine of the product. Every module uses the same chain of records:

   Regulatory source  >  Obligation  >  Legal entity  >  Risk  >  Control
      >  Monitoring rule  >  Alert or issue  >  Action  >  Evidence  >  Assurance conclusion

Why it matters: when a regulator asks "how do you know you comply with this rule, in this
country?", you can follow one line from the rule itself to the evidence and the conclusion.
No separate spreadsheets per module or per country, so nothing has to be reconciled.

In the app: Obligations and returns, open any obligation, section "Traceability".

A4. THE EIGHT IDEAS TO GET ACROSS
---------------------------------
1. One platform, one chain of records (A2), across every entity and jurisdiction.
2. Regulatory change becomes action. A new rule is assessed per legal entity, mapped to the
   obligations, controls and policies it affects, and turned into actions with owners.
3. Continuous, not periodic. Automatic checks on live data, not quarterly spreadsheets.
4. Evidence, not opinion. Control ratings come from test results; self-assessments are checked
   against them.
5. Accountability. Every alert and action has an owner, a deadline and an escalation path.
6. Separation of duties. Nobody approves their own work: alert closure (four-eyes), regulatory
   change approval (maker-checker), policy approval (author cannot approve), SAR submission
   (MLRO only), EDD (different senior approver).
7. Need-to-know access. Each role sees only what it needs; business unit managers see only
   their unit; the administrator sees personal data masked.
8. Human in control of automation. Requirements drafted automatically from regulations must be
   validated by a person; risk scores show every contributing factor; a person decides.


A5. TOUR OF EVERY SCREEN (in sidebar order: the 14 Version 1.2 sections)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each screen: what it is, who uses it, what to show, one line to say.

1. EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE

Overview
   What: top figures (critical alerts, open alerts, rules passing, open AML cases, returns due);
   NEW: "Group exposure by legal entity": each entity with its jurisdiction, open regulatory
   changes, open alerts, high risks, failing controls, open cases and assurance rating; then
   alert trend, recent activity, control monitoring, risks by category, top residual risks.
   Who: CRO, CCO, Head of Audit, Board.
   Say: "The whole group's exposure, entity by entity, in one view."

2. PERIMETER INTELLIGENCE (new)

Coverage and sources
   What: three tabs.
   - Coverage: jurisdiction packs (UK, Nigeria, UAE, EU/Ireland, international standards) with
     coverage, regulators, how suspicious activity is reported there (for example "SAR to NCA"
     in the UK, "STR to NFIU within 24 hours" in Nigeria), breach notification deadlines, and
     local language, currency, time zone and data residency. Packs for Kenya, Ghana and South
     Africa are "available" to switch on. Plus the legal entities table and the regulatory
     horizon (open changes and future effective dates).
   - Sources: the inventory of authoritative sources with provenance: where each came from,
     version, when it was retrieved and checked, and a fingerprint of the exact version used.
   - Cross-border comparison: how the same requirement differs by country, and whether the
     group's existing control can be reused, needs a local variant, or is a gap.
   Top figures include content freshness (days since sources were checked) and quality.
   Who: CCO, regulatory counsel, CRO, Board (read only).
   Say: "We know what the rules are in every country we operate in, where they came from, and
   how fresh our copy is."

Regulatory change
   What: each regulatory change moves through: Capture, Validate extraction, Assess
   applicability, Map impact and actions, Submit, Approve (different person). Opening a change
   shows the summary, source and provenance, "Extracted requirements" (drafted automatically;
   each needs "Validate"), "Applicability by legal entity" ("Assess"), "Impact" (obligations,
   controls, policies, risks: "Edit mapping"), "Actions" ("Add action") and "History".
   Buttons: "Triage", "Submit for approval"; for the approver: "Approve" or "Return to
   preparer". The person who prepared it cannot approve it (maker-checker). Submission is
   refused until every requirement is validated, every entity assessed and the impact mapped.
   Who: Compliance Officer prepares (maker); CCO approves (checker).
   Say: "A new rule becomes owned actions, per entity, with a second pair of eyes."

3. REGULATORY OBLIGATIONS

Obligations and returns
   What: Obligations tab: laws and rules the group must meet, each with status, mapped controls,
   evidence and, NEW, the "Traceability" chain from source to assurance conclusion with the
   reasons for that conclusion. Regulatory returns tab: reports due, "Generate" and "Confirm
   submission".
   Who: CCO, Compliance Officer.
   Say: "Every obligation traces to its source, its controls, and whether we can prove it."

Policies
   What: lifecycle (draft, submit, approve or return, new version, retire) and acknowledgement
   tracking. Authors cannot approve their own policy.
   Who: CCO approves; everyone acknowledges.

4. ENTERPRISE RISK

Risk register
   What: 5x5 heat map, Inherent vs Residual. Residual is calculated from tested control
   effectiveness. KRIs with amber and red limits; above red raises an alert.
   Who: CRO edits; others read.
   Say: "The gap between inherent and residual is what your controls are worth."

5. CONTROLS AND CCM

Alerts: every alert with owner, deadlines, escalation path, timeline and evidence;
   four-eyes closure ("Request closure", then someone else "Sign off closure").
Control monitoring: the 12 automatic control rules (CCM-001 to CCM-012), with "Configure"
   (reason required), "New rule", "Run now".
Controls: the control library; "Record test result" recalculates effectiveness.
Access reviews: privileged accounts recertified every 90 days (CISO).
Vetting: background checks for new starters (HR).

6. FINANCIAL CRIME

Customers and screening
   What: Customers tab (KYC status, risk rating, PEP and sanctions; "Verify KYC", "Refresh KYC",
   "Start EDD", "Complete EDD", "Re-screen") and Held payments tab (high-value payments waiting
   for sanctions screening; "Screen": clear, or hit which opens a case).
   Who: MLRO, AML Analyst.

7. TRANSACTION MONITORING (new, planned capability)

Alert queues and scenarios
   What: three tabs.
   - Alert queue: fiat and crypto alerts from all channels and entities (filter All, Fiat,
     Crypto). Opening an alert shows the customer's usual pattern next to what triggered the
     alert, the linked transactions (in local currency: naira, dirham, euro, pound), the
     customer, entity and scenario. Buttons: "Assign to me", "Close, no case" (rationale, can be
     marked false positive), "Escalate to case" (opens a case with an explained score and
     notifies the MLRO).
   - Scenarios: 11 rules and behavioural analytics (structuring, velocity, pass-through, money
     mule indicators, dormant accounts, high-risk geography, behavioural change, mobile money
     agent cash-out, round-amount remittances, crypto off-ramp) with alerts, false-positive
     rate, conversion to case and last tuned. "Tune" needs a reason and is audited.
   - Channels: bank transfers, cards, mobile money, remittance, e-wallets, crypto; mode
     (real-time, near-real-time, batch) and volumes.
   Who: AML Analyst (starts here), MLRO, fraud teams.
   Say: "Bank, card, mobile money, remittance and crypto in one queue, feeding one case model."

8. DIGITAL ASSETS INTELLIGENCE (new, planned capability)

Wallets and Travel Rule
   What: four tabs.
   - Transfers and tracing: crypto transfers with risk; selecting one shows the path of funds
     (for example: mixing service, intermediate wallet, then the customer's deposit address).
     "Escalate to case" or "Clear transfer".
   - Wallets: screened wallets with exposure categories (sanctioned, mixer, darknet,
     ransomware, scam, stolen funds, high-risk services, gambling) and status.
   - Travel Rule: transfers missing originator or beneficiary information, unhosted wallets,
     counterparty VASPs; "Request info", "VASP due diligence", "Release", "Reject". Release is
     refused until the counterparty VASP's due diligence is complete.
   - Exposure: wallets by risk category.
   "Screen a wallet" gives an illustrative result (no analytics provider is connected).
   Who: MLRO, crypto compliance, investigators.
   Say: "Specialist blockchain data comes from approved providers; OysterChecks keeps the
   score, the decision, the evidence and the SAR or STR."

9. CASES AND INVESTIGATIONS

Cases
   What: one queue for every investigation: from screening, transaction monitoring and digital
   assets (filter by type). SAR pipeline (New, Analyst review, MLRO review, SAR decided,
   Submitted to NCA). Each case: score breakdown, customer, transactions, evidence;
   "Decide to file SAR" and "Confirm NCA submission" for the MLRO only.
   Who: MLRO (starts here), AML Analyst.
   Say: "Fiat and crypto in one case, one decision, one audit trail."

10. THIRD PARTY RISK: Third parties: automatic tiering, due diligence, review cadence, exit.

11. INCIDENTS AND REMEDIATION: Incidents and actions: root cause, links, regulator deadlines,
    corrective actions, gated closure.

12. THREE LINES ASSURANCE: Assurance map (coverage, gaps and over-assurance across the three
    lines), Internal audit (engagements and findings), Self-assessments (flagged when they
    disagree with test results).

13. EVIDENCE AND REPORTING: Board report (written from live figures, "Print / save PDF") and
    ESG (TCFD, CSRD, GRI metrics with evidence).

14. ADMINISTRATION AND INTEGRATIONS
   Integrations: connected systems ("simulated") plus the Version 1.2 connectors marked
      "planned": regulatory content sources, card processor, mobile money platform, blockchain
      analytics provider, Travel Rule network.
   Users, Audit log ("Verify integrity").
   Delivery programme (internal): ONLY Admin and Board see it. Status pre-initiation;
      governance gates 0 to 4 ("Record decision"; each needs the previous one approved); six
      provisional phases locked until Gate 4; the five MVP journeys. Do NOT show this screen to
      customers; it is for delivery and programme governance.

C1. QUESTIONS YOU WILL GET, AND HONEST ANSWERS
----------------------------------------------
   Q: Is this live? Are customers using it?
   A: No. It is a working demonstration of the intended platform on illustrative data. The
      programme is at pre-initiation: the next step is Gate 0, programme authority.

   Q: Is transaction monitoring or blockchain monitoring operational?
   A: No, both are planned capabilities. They become operational only after data-provider
      contracts, integrations, scenarios, thresholds, model governance, testing and regulatory
      validation are complete.

   Q: Which blockchain analytics provider do you use?
   A: None is selected yet. Wallet attribution and tracing will come from approved providers
      through APIs; OysterChecks keeps the risk score, alerts, investigation, evidence,
      decisions and the SAR or STR.

   Q: Where does the regulatory content come from? Is it legal advice?
   A: From authoritative sources (legislation, regulator handbooks, standards), curated into
      version-controlled jurisdiction packs with provenance. In this demo the summaries are
      simplified and illustrative; they are not legal advice.

   Q: Does AI write our obligations?
   A: Automated extraction drafts requirements from the source text. A person must validate
      every one before the assessment can be submitted, and a different person approves it.

   Q: Can it handle Nigeria? Other African countries?
   A: The demo has active packs for the UK, Nigeria (CBN, NFIU, NDPC, SEC Nigeria), the UAE and
      the EU (Ireland), and available packs for Kenya, Ghana and South Africa. Local reporting
      rules differ: SAR to the NCA in the UK, STR to the NFIU within 24 hours in Nigeria.

   Q: Why are the amounts in the main case flow in pounds?
   A: The most detailed demo records belong to the UK entity. Monitoring alerts show local
      currency (naira, dirham, euro), with the pound equivalent.

   Q: Single sign-on, two-step verification, email and SMS?
   A: Planned for production. The demo only checks the demo password and sends in-app
      notifications.

   Q: Can we host it ourselves?
   A: The target architecture supports multi-tenant SaaS, dedicated, private cloud, hybrid and
      on-premises, with regional hosting and data-residency controls.


C2. GLOSSARY
------------
   Perimeter Intelligence  Regulatory intelligence across jurisdictions, inside OysterChecks.
   Jurisdiction pack       Curated set of a country's regulators, sources and obligations.
   Provenance              Where a source came from, which version, and when it was checked.
   Applicability           Whether a rule applies to a given legal entity, and why.
   Maker-checker           The preparer of a decision cannot also approve it.
   Legal entity            A separately regulated company within the group.
   Traceability chain      Source > obligation > entity > risk > control > rule > alert > action
                           > evidence > assurance conclusion.
   CCM                     Continuous control monitoring.
   Scenario                A transaction monitoring rule or behavioural model.
   False positive          An alert that needed no action.
   Structuring             Splitting deposits to stay below a reporting threshold.
   Pass-through            Funds that come in and go straight out again.
   Money mule              An account used to move other people's illicit funds.
   SAR / STR               Suspicious activity / transaction report.
   NCA / NFIU              UK National Crime Agency / Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit.
   MLRO                    Money laundering reporting officer.
   VASP                    Virtual asset service provider (for example a crypto exchange).
   Travel Rule             Originator and beneficiary information must travel with transfers.
   Unhosted wallet         A crypto wallet not held at a VASP.
   Mixer                   A service that obscures where crypto funds came from.
   Tracing                 Following funds across wallets and blockchains.
   Cross-chain             A transfer that moves between blockchains through a bridge.
   KYC / KYB / EDD / PEP   Know your customer / business; enhanced due diligence; politically
                           exposed person.
   Inherent / residual     Risk before / after controls (residual uses tested effectiveness).
   KRI                     Key risk indicator with amber and red limits.
   Three lines             Management; risk and compliance; internal audit (plus external
                           audit and regulators).
   Pre-initiation          The programme has not formally started.
   Gate                    A governance decision that must be approved before moving on.


C3. PRACTICAL TIPS
------------------
