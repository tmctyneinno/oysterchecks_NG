OYSTERCHECKS: HOW TO PRESENT THE PLATFORM
==========================================

The idea in one sentence
------------------------
Tell one story: a problem shows up in the data, the platform catches it, the right
people deal with it, and the board sees the result. Every screen you show should be
a step in that story.


1. BEFORE THE DEMO (about 15 minutes)
-------------------------------------

a) Open the demo
   - Hosted: open the link where you uploaded the site.
   - On your laptop:
         cd frontend
         npm run dev            (opens on http://localhost:3000)
     or, to run the exact files you will upload:
         npm run generate
         npm run preview

b) Start from clean data
   On the sign-in page, open "Demo accounts" and click "Reset demo data".
   (When signed in, the same reset is the circular-arrow button in the top bar.)
   Dates always count from today, so "overdue" and "due in 3 days" look right
   whenever you present.

c) Open TWO TABS in the SAME browser window, side by side.
   Sign in as a different person in each, for example the CRO in one tab and
   the CCO in the other. Changes made in one tab appear in the other a moment
   later. This is how you show live updates and the "two people must approve" rule.
   Do NOT use an incognito window for the second person: incognito does not share
   the demo data with the normal window.

d) Use the light theme on a projector. Switch to dark once (the half-circle icon
   in the top bar) to show it exists.

e) Set the browser zoom to 110 to 125 percent so tables are readable from the back
   of the room.

f) Do one full rehearsal, then click "Reset demo data" again before the real session.

2. THE WALKTHROUGH (about 25 minutes)
-------------------------------------

STEP 1. Set up the problem (1 minute, no clicking)
   Say: "Most organisations find out a control failed weeks later, from a
   spreadsheet someone filled in. OysterChecks reads the source systems directly
   and tells you within minutes."

STEP 2. Overview, signed in as the CRO (2 minutes)
   - Walk across the top figures: critical alerts, rules passing, open AML cases,
     regulatory returns due.
   - Point at "Control monitoring": 12 automated checks watching live data.
   - Point at "Highest residual risks": risk after counting how well the controls
     actually performed in testing.
   - Point at the green "Live" dot in the top bar: nobody refreshes this page,
     it updates itself.
   - Point at the activity feed: every action anyone takes appears here.

STEP 3. Control monitoring (3 minutes)
   - Show the rule cards: what each rule watches, where its data comes from, how
     fast a breach must be fixed, and who it escalates to.
   - Open a failing rule and point out its escalation chain
     (for example Compliance, then MLRO, then CCO, then CRO).
   - Click "Configure" (as the CCO): you cannot change a control without giving a
     reason, and the old and new values go into the permanent audit log.

STEP 4. One alert from start to finish (5 minutes). THIS IS THE CENTREPIECE.
   - Go to "Alerts" and open "Unscreened high-value transaction" (the GBP 340,000
     wire from Hadejia Trading Ltd to Okoya Bay Holdings in the British Virgin
     Islands).
   - Show the escalation path, the deadline and the timeline.
   - Click "Acknowledge", then "Remediation plan".
   - Attach a file. Point out the fingerprint (SHA-256) shown next to it: proof the
     file has not been changed since it was attached.
   - Click "Request closure" and write the evidence.
   - Try to sign it off yourself. The system refuses: two people must approve
     (the "four-eyes" rule).
   - In the second tab, signed in as the CCO, open the same alert and sign off the
     closure. Point out that the first tab updates by itself.
   - Say: "This is the difference between a ticket system and an assurance system.
     Every step has an owner, a deadline and evidence."

STEP 5. Financial crime, signed in as the MLRO (4 minutes)
   - "Held payments" tab: screen the GBP 340k payment as a "Hit". A case opens
     automatically with a risk score.
   - Open the case and show the score breakdown: "No black box. Every point of the
     score is explained, and a person must review before any decision."
   - Click "Decide to file SAR": the report draft fills itself in from the case
     data, and the 7-day deadline to the National Crime Agency starts.
   - Sign in as the AML Analyst (in a tab) and show they CANNOT submit the report.
     Only the MLRO can. Then submit it as the MLRO with an NCA reference.

STEP 6. Risk register (3 minutes)
   - Switch the heat map between "Inherent" and "Residual": "Same risks, before
     and after controls. The difference is what your controls are worth."
   - Open a risk to show its linked controls and their tested effectiveness.
   - Record a key risk indicator (KRI) value above its red limit: an alert is raised
     to the risk owner straight away (check the Alerts count in the sidebar).

STEP 7. Controls and assurance (3 minutes)
   - Controls: "Effectiveness comes from test results, not from people marking their
     own work." Record two failed tests on an effective control and watch its rating
     drop to ineffective, then show the linked risk's residual score go up.
   - Self-assessments: show where a manager's own rating disagrees with the test
     results. It is flagged.
   - Assurance map: the three lines of defence side by side, with gaps and
     duplicated effort flagged automatically.

STEP 8. Compliance, policies and incidents (3 minutes)
   - Compliance: obligations linked to controls, the evidence pack download, and the
     regulatory returns countdown. "Generate" a return, then "Submit" it.
   - Policies: "The person who wrote a policy cannot approve it." Show acknowledgement
     tracking (how many staff have read and accepted each policy).
   - Incidents: open one with a regulator deadline (for example the 72-hour data
     protection breach window). It cannot be closed until the root cause, all
     corrective actions and the regulator notice are done.

STEP 9. Who sees what (2 minutes)
   - Sign in as the Retail business unit manager: they see only their own unit's
     alerts and self-assessments, nothing else.
   - Sign in as Admin: customer names and personal details are masked.
   - Sign in as Board: read-only overview plus the board report.
   - Say: "Access rules are checked on every request, not just hidden in the screen."

STEP 10. Board report and trust (2 minutes)
   - Board report: a written summary generated from the live figures. Every sentence
     traces back to a number on the page. Click "Print / save PDF".
   - Audit log (as Admin or CRO): click "Verify integrity". "Every action is chained
     to the one before it. If anyone edited history, this check would fail." Point
     out that the actions you took during the demo are already there.

STEP 11. Close (1 minute)
   - Integrations: the systems it connects to (HR, core banking, CRM, ERP, Active
     Directory, sanctions lists).
   - Implementation: the 26-week rollout plan with sign-off gates.
   - Final line: "Real-time, evidence-based assurance, from the first alert to the
     board pack."


3. SHORTER VERSIONS FOR DIFFERENT AUDIENCES
-------------------------------------------
   - Executives (10 minutes): steps 2, 4 (shortened), 9 and 10.
     Focus on visibility, accountability and board reporting.
   - Risk and compliance teams (30+ minutes): the full walkthrough, slowing down on
     steps 4 to 8.
   - IT and security: steps 3, 4, 9 and 10, plus how it connects to their systems
     (Integrations page) and the audit trail.


4. QUESTIONS YOU WILL GET, AND HONEST ANSWERS
---------------------------------------------
   Q: Is this live bank data?
   A: No. It is realistic demo data. In production the platform reads from your
      core banking, CRM, HR and identity systems (the Integrations page shows them
      as "simulated" here).

   Q: Does it send emails or text messages?
   A: In-app notifications work in the demo. Email and SMS are planned for the
      production version.

   Q: Single sign-on with our identity provider? Two-step verification?
   A: Planned for production (SAML or OIDC, plus authenticator-app codes). The demo
      only checks the demo password.

   Q: Can we change the rules and thresholds?
   A: Yes, from the Configure screen on Control monitoring, and every change is
      recorded in the audit log with a reason.

   Q: Is the AML score AI?
   A: It is a transparent weighted model on purpose, so regulators and analysts can
      see exactly why a case scored what it did. A machine-learning model can sit
      behind the same screen later.

   Q: Where is the data stored?
   A: In this demo, only in your browser. In production, in your own database with
      evidence files in tamper-proof storage.


5. PRACTICAL TIPS
-----------------
   - If something gets messy during practice, click "Reset demo data" and you are
     back to the starting point in a second.
   - Changes are saved in the browser you used. A different computer or browser
     starts from the clean demo data.
   - Uploaded files can be downloaded again only in the tab that uploaded them;
     other tabs show the file name and fingerprint.
   - Keep the second tab already signed in as the CCO before you start step 4, so the
     four-eyes moment flows without typing.
   - Have the board report open in a spare tab in case you run short of time and
     want to jump straight to the ending.
