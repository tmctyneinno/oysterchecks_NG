PART A. UNDERSTAND THE PLATFORM
==========================================================================================

A1. WHAT OYSTERCHECKS IS
------------------------
OysterChecks is a governance, risk and compliance (GRC) platform for banks, payment
companies and other regulated financial firms.

The problem it solves: in most firms, risk and compliance teams find out that something
went wrong weeks later, from spreadsheets and emails that people fill in by hand.
Controls are marked "effective" because someone said so. Evidence is scattered.
Nobody can prove who did what, and the board gets a report that is already out of date.

What OysterChecks does instead:
   - It connects to the firm's own systems (core banking, CRM, HR, identity, ERP,
     sanctions lists) and checks the data automatically, all the time.
   - When a check fails, it raises an alert, gives it an owner and a deadline, and
     escalates it up the chain if nobody acts.
   - People resolve the alert with evidence, and a second person must approve closure.
   - Risk scores, control ratings and compliance status update from real results,
     not from opinions.
   - The board report is written from live figures.
   - Every action is recorded in a tamper-evident audit log.

Who uses it: the Chief Risk Officer, Chief Compliance Officer, MLRO (money laundering
lead) and AML analysts, compliance officers, internal audit, the CISO, HR, business unit
managers, the board, and a platform administrator. Each sees a different starting screen
and only what their role allows.


A2. HOW IT WORKS (THE FLOW)
---------------------------

   Source systems        Core banking, CRM, HR, Active Directory, ERP, sanctions lists
         |
         v
   Automatic checks      12 control monitoring rules run continuously
         |
         v
   Alerts                Owner, deadline (SLA), escalation path
         |
         v
   People act            Acknowledge, plan the fix, attach evidence, request closure
         |
         v
   Four-eyes sign-off    A second authorised person approves or rejects the closure
         |
         v
   Registers update      Risks, controls, compliance, assurance, incidents
         |
         v
   Board report          Written from the live figures, printable as PDF

   Underneath everything: the audit log records every action and can be verified.


A3. THE SIX IDEAS TO GET ACROSS
-------------------------------
1. Continuous, not periodic. Checks run against live data, not quarterly spreadsheets.
2. Evidence, not opinion. A control is "effective" because its tests passed, not because
   its owner said so. Self-assessments are shown next to test results, never instead.
3. Accountability. Every alert has an owner, a deadline and an escalation path.
4. Separation of duties. Nobody approves their own work: alert closures need a second
   person, policy authors cannot approve their own policy, only the MLRO can submit a
   suspicious activity report, and EDD needs a different senior approver.
5. Need-to-know access. Each role sees only what it needs. Business unit managers see
   only their own unit. The administrator sees customer and staff details masked.
   Access is checked on every request, not just hidden on screen.
6. Proof. Every action goes into an audit log where each entry is chained to the one
   before it, so any tampering would be detected.


A4. TOUR OF EVERY SCREEN (in sidebar order)
-------------------------------------------
For each screen: what it is, who mainly uses it, what to show, and one line to say.

GENERAL

Overview
   What: the executive dashboard. Top figures (critical alerts, open alerts, rules
   passing, open AML cases, regulatory returns due), alerts raised over 14 days, recent
   activity, control monitoring status, risks by category, highest residual risks.
   Who: CRO, CCO, Head of Audit, Board.
   Say: "One screen to see the firm's risk position right now, not last quarter."

Alerts
   What: every alert raised by the automatic checks, KRIs or people. Filter by severity,
   switch between open, all and closed, assign several at once, export to CSV, or
   "Raise alert" manually. Opening an alert shows the escalation path, deadlines,
   timeline and evidence, with the buttons Acknowledge, Assign, Add note, Remediation
   plan, Escalate, Request closure, and for a second person, Sign off closure or Reject
   closure.
   Who: everyone who handles issues.
   Say: "Every issue has an owner, a deadline and a full history."

Board report
   What: a board pack written automatically from live figures: key numbers, top risks,
   failing rules, overdue alerts, non-compliant obligations, returns due and assurance
   coverage. "Print / save PDF" produces the paper version.
   Who: CRO, CCO, Head of Audit, Board.
   Say: "The board pack writes itself, and every sentence traces back to a number."

MONITORING

Control monitoring
   What: the 12 automatic checks, each with a status light (passing, near threshold,
   failing), what it watches, its data source, its deadline and who it escalates to.
   The 12 checks are:
      CCM-001 Unverified onboarding (no verified KYC 24 hours after account opening)
      CCM-002 Unscreened high-value transaction (payment over the threshold not screened)
      CCM-003 SAR overdue (suspicious activity report not filed within 7 days)
      CCM-004 EDD incomplete for a high-risk customer
      CCM-005 KYC refresh overdue
      CCM-006 New starter without vetting
      CCM-007 Privileged access not reviewed in 90 days
      CCM-008 Regulatory return due
      CCM-009 Self-assessment overdue
      CCM-010 Audit recommendation overdue
      CCM-011 Unusual transaction volume
      CCM-012 Control failed twice in a row
   The CCO and Admin can "Configure" a rule (a reason is required and recorded),
   add a "New rule" and "Run now".
   Who: CCO, CISO, Compliance.
   Say: "These are your controls being tested every minute, not once a year."

Controls
   What: the control library. Each control shows its owner, type, frequency, whether it
   is a key control or automated, its linked monitoring rule, its test history and pass
   rate, and its effectiveness. "Record test result" adds a test; the rating is
   recalculated: last three tests passed = effective, last two failed = ineffective,
   otherwise partially effective. Export available.
   Who: CCO and CISO record tests; others read.
   Say: "Effectiveness comes from test results, not from people marking their own work."

Integrations
   What: the eight connected systems (HR, core banking, CRM, ERP, Active Directory,
   sanctions database, audit management, document management), with last sync time,
   records received and data quality. In the demo they are labelled "simulated".
   Admin can "Sync now".
   Who: Admin, IT.
   Say: "It reads from the systems you already have. Nobody re-keys data."

RISK AND COMPLIANCE

Risk register
   What: all open risks on a 5 by 5 heat map you can switch between Inherent (before
   controls) and Residual (after controls). Residual risk is calculated from how well
   the linked controls performed in testing. Each risk has key risk indicators (KRIs)
   with amber and red limits. The CRO can "Add risk", "Mark reviewed" and
   "Escalate to board" (which notifies the board), and record KRI values. A KRI above
   its red limit raises an alert immediately.
   Who: CRO (edits); others read.
   Say: "The gap between inherent and residual is what your controls are actually worth."

Compliance
   What: two tabs. Obligations: the laws and rules the firm must meet, each linked to
   controls and policies, with a status (compliant, partial, non-compliant, not
   assessed) and an evidence pack download. Regulatory returns: the reports due to
   regulators with countdowns. "Generate" builds a return from platform data and
   "Confirm submission" records it with the regulator's reference.
   Who: CCO (everything), Compliance Officer (obligations and generating returns).
   Say: "Every obligation is tied to the controls that prove you meet it."

Policies
   What: the policy library with a full lifecycle: draft, "Submit for approval",
   "Approve" or "Return to owner", "Start new version", "Retire". Staff confirm with
   "I have read and accept this policy", and the screen shows how many have accepted.
   The author of a policy can never approve it.
   Who: CCO approves; everyone acknowledges.
   Say: "You can prove who has read which version of every policy."

ESG
   What: environmental, social and governance metrics under TCFD, CSRD and GRI
   (emissions, renewable energy, gender pay gap, board independence, training,
   supplier modern slavery attestations). "Record value" needs an evidence source,
   and the status (on track, at risk, off track) is calculated against the target.
   Who: CCO.
   Say: "ESG figures with their evidence, ready for the annual report."

ASSURANCE

Assurance map
   What: all assurance work across the three lines of defence: first line (business
   management), second line (risk and compliance), third line (internal audit,
   external audit, regulators). It shows coverage per control, flags controls with no
   assurance (gaps) and controls checked too often (over-assurance). "Plan activity"
   adds new work.
   Who: Head of Audit, CRO, CCO.
   Say: "One map of who checks what, so nothing is missed and nothing is checked twice."

Internal audit
   What: audit engagements moving through planned, fieldwork, reporting and closed
   (with an overall rating), and audit findings rated high, medium or low with an owner
   and due date. Owners write a "Management response"; only internal audit can
   "Change due date" or close a finding. A finding can raise an incident.
   Overdue recommendations trigger rule CCM-010.
   Who: Head of Audit.
   Say: "Audit findings are tracked to closure, and overdue ones escalate on their own."

Self-assessments
   What: business unit managers rate their own controls each period. A rating below
   "effective" needs an explanation. Where a manager's rating disagrees with the test
   results, it is flagged.
   Who: business unit managers submit; audit and risk review.
   Say: "We ask managers, but we check their answers against the evidence."

FINANCIAL CRIME

Cases and screening
   What: three tabs. Cases: AML investigations with a risk score where every point is
   explained, assigned to an analyst, escalated to the MLRO, then dismissed or reported.
   "Decide to file SAR" (MLRO only) drafts the suspicious activity report from case
   data and starts a 7-day clock; "Confirm NCA submission" (MLRO only) records it.
   Held payments: high-value payments waiting for sanctions screening; "Screen" as clear
   or hit, where a hit opens a case automatically. Customers: KYC status, risk rating,
   PEP and sanctions flags, with "Verify KYC", "Refresh KYC", "Start EDD",
   "Complete EDD" (needs a different senior approver) and "Re-screen".
   Who: MLRO and AML Analysts.
   Say: "No black box: every score is explained and a person decides."

OPERATIONS

Incidents
   What: incidents and corrective actions. "Log incident", record the root cause
   (5 whys or fishbone), "Link records" to risks, controls and obligations,
   "Mark notifiable" (which sets the regulator and the legal deadline, for example
   72 hours for a personal data breach), "Add action" for corrective actions, and
   "Close incident" with lessons learned. Closure is blocked until the root cause, all
   actions and any regulator notice are done.
   Who: CCO, Compliance, CISO, business unit managers (log, but cannot close).
   Say: "From the first report to lessons learned, with the regulator deadline on screen."

Third parties
   What: the supplier register. "Onboard supplier" classifies it automatically as
   critical, high, medium or low based on data access, spend, country and importance.
   "Complete due diligence", "Record periodic review" (every 12, 18, 24 or 36 months
   by tier), monitoring flags, "Exit supplier".
   Who: CCO, Compliance.
   Say: "Your outsourcing risk is tiered and reviewed on schedule."

Vetting
   What: background checks for new starters (BPSS, DBS, BS7858), with start dates.
   Anyone due to start without clearance is flagged, and a failed check notifies the
   CISO so no system access is given. Rule CCM-006 watches this.
   Who: HR.
   Say: "Nobody gets access before they are cleared."

Access reviews
   What: privileged system accounts that must be recertified every 90 days.
   "Recertify" with a decision to keep or remove access. Rule CCM-007 watches this.
   Who: CISO.
   Say: "Powerful accounts are reviewed on time, with proof."

DELIVERY

Implementation
   What: the rollout plan: six phases over 26 weeks, each with tasks and a sign-off gate.
   A phase can only be signed off when all its tasks are done and the previous phase
   is signed off.
   Who: Admin edits; executives read.
   Say: "A clear, gated path from contract to go-live."

ADMIN

Users
   What: user accounts: "Add user", change role or business unit, deactivate, "Unlock",
   "Reset MFA". The administrator cannot change their own role.
   Who: Admin.

Audit log
   What: every user action and system event, searchable, kept for 7 years.
   "Verify integrity" checks the whole chain.
   Who: Admin, CRO, CCO, Head of Audit.
   Say: "If anyone tried to change history, this check would fail."

ALWAYS AVAILABLE (top bar)
   Search across alerts, risks, controls, policies and more; the "Live" indicator;
   light and dark theme; notifications bell; reset demo data; sign out.

A6. GLOSSARY
------------
   GRC           Governance, risk and compliance.
   CCM           Continuous control monitoring: automatic checks on live data.
   SLA           The deadline to acknowledge and resolve an alert.
   Escalation    Moving an alert up a chain of people when its deadline passes.
   Four-eyes     A second person must approve; nobody signs off their own work.
   Inherent risk Risk before any controls.
   Residual risk Risk remaining after controls, based on how well they tested.
   KRI           Key risk indicator: a measured figure with amber and red limits.
   Control       A process or check that reduces a risk.
   Three lines   1st line: business managers. 2nd line: risk and compliance.
                 3rd line: internal audit (plus external audit and regulators).
   CSA           Control self-assessment: managers rate their own controls.
   AML           Anti-money laundering.
   KYC / EDD     Know your customer / enhanced due diligence for high-risk customers.
   PEP           Politically exposed person.
   SAR           Suspicious activity report, filed with the financial intelligence unit.
   MLRO          Money laundering reporting officer, the only person who files SARs.
   NCA           National Crime Agency (the UK body that receives SARs).
   ESG           Environmental, social and governance reporting.
   TPRM          Third-party (supplier) risk management.
   SHA-256       A file fingerprint; any change to the file changes the fingerprint.

B1. BEFORE THE DEMO (about 15 minutes)
--------------------------------------
a) Open the demo: the hosted link, or on your laptop:
         cd frontend
         npm run dev            (opens on http://localhost:3000)

b) Start from clean data: on the sign-in page open "Demo accounts" and click
   "Reset demo data". (When signed in, it is the circular-arrow button in the top bar.)
   Dates always count from today, so "overdue" and "due in 3 days" read correctly.

c) Open TWO TABS in the SAME browser window, side by side:
      Tab 1: CRO  (cro@oysterchecks.demo)
      Tab 2: CCO  (cco@oysterchecks.demo)
   Changes in one tab appear in the other a moment later. Do NOT use an incognito
   window for the second person: incognito does not share the demo data.
   On the sign-in page, "Demo accounts" fills the email and password with one click.

d) Light theme on a projector (half-circle icon in the top bar). Browser zoom 110 to
   125 percent.

e) Rehearse once, then click "Reset demo data" again before the real session.


B2. THE WALKTHROUGH (about 30 minutes)
--------------------------------------
Each step says which tab or account to use.

STEP 1. The problem (1 minute, no clicking)
   Say: "Most firms find out a control failed weeks later, from a spreadsheet someone
   filled in. OysterChecks reads your systems directly and tells you within minutes,
   with an owner, a deadline and evidence for every issue."

STEP 2. Overview  [Tab 1, CRO]  (2 minutes)
   - The top figures: critical alerts, open alerts, rules passing, open AML cases,
     returns due.
   - "Control monitoring" panel: the 12 automatic checks.
   - "Highest residual risks": risk after counting how controls performed in tests.
   - The "Live" dot: the page updates itself.
   - "Recent activity": every action appears here.

STEP 3. Control monitoring  [Tab 2, CCO]  (3 minutes)
   - Open "Control monitoring". Show the rule cards: what each watches, its data
     source, its deadline, and who it escalates to.
   - Point at a failing rule and its escalation chain (for example Compliance, then
     MLRO, then CCO, then CRO).
   - Click "Configure" on a rule: a reason is required, and the before and after
     values go into the audit log. Cancel or save.

STEP 4. One alert from start to finish  [Tab 1, CRO, then Tab 2, CCO]  (5 minutes)
   THIS IS THE CENTREPIECE.
   - Tab 1: "Alerts", open "Unscreened high-value transaction: TX-0001348" (a
     GBP 340,000 payment from Hadejia Trading Ltd to Okoya Bay Holdings in the British
     Virgin Islands, not yet sanctions-screened).
   - Show the escalation path, the deadlines and the timeline.
   - Click "Acknowledge", then "Remediation plan" and write a short plan.
   - Attach a file. Point at the SHA-256 fingerprint: proof the file is unchanged.
   - Click "Request closure" and describe the evidence.
   - Point out there is no sign-off button for you: you requested the closure, so
     someone else must approve it (four-eyes).
   - Tab 2 (CCO): open the same alert, click "Sign off closure". Go back to Tab 1:
     it shows "Closed" by itself, with Daniel Okafor as the approver.
   - Say: "This is the difference between a ticket system and an assurance system."

STEP 5. Financial crime  [sign Tab 2 in as MLRO]  (4 minutes)
   - Sign out of Tab 2 and sign in as the MLRO (mlro@oysterchecks.demo).
   - "Held payments" tab: "Screen" the GBP 340,000 payment as a hit. A case opens
     automatically.
   - Open the new case: the score breakdown explains every point.
   - Click "Decide to file SAR": the report draft fills itself in, and the 7-day clock
     starts.
   - Optional: in Tab 1, sign in as the AML Analyst and open the case: there is no
     way for them to submit it. Only the MLRO can.
   - Back as the MLRO: "Confirm NCA submission" with a reference such as
     NCA-2026-00123.

STEP 6. Risk register  [Tab 1, CRO]  (3 minutes)
   - Switch the heat map between "Inherent" and "Residual".
   - Open a risk: linked controls with their tested effectiveness, and its KRIs.
   - Record a KRI value above its red limit (for example "Unscreened payments >£10k
     (24h)" on R-001): an alert is raised immediately; the Alerts count goes up.

STEP 7. Controls and assurance  [Tab 2, sign in as CCO again]  (4 minutes)
   - "Controls": open C-002 ("Real-time sanctions screening of outbound payments",
     currently effective). "Record test result" as Fail twice. It drops to
     "Ineffective".
   - Tab 1 (CRO), "Risk register": R-001's residual score has gone up (from 5 to 15),
     and the Overview's top risks update too.
   - "Assurance map": the three lines of defence side by side. Point at the
     over-assurance flag (a control checked more often than needed) and explain that
     a control with no assurance at all would be flagged as a gap the same way.
     (Self-assessments are shown live in step 10, as the Retail manager.)
   - "Internal audit": engagements and findings; overdue recommendations escalate
     through rule CCM-010.

STEP 8. Compliance, policies and incidents  [Tab 2, CCO]  (4 minutes)
   - "Compliance", Obligations: each linked to controls; download the evidence pack.
   - "Compliance", Regulatory returns: "Generate" a return, then "Confirm submission"
     with a reference.
   - "Policies": open POL-004 (in review, written by the CCO) and click "Approve": it is
     refused because authors cannot approve their own policy. Show the acknowledgement
     counts.
   - "Incidents": open INC-0102 "Customer data emailed to wrong recipient". It is
     notifiable to the ICO within 72 hours; the deadline is on screen. Explain that
     "Close incident" is blocked until the root cause, actions and regulator notice
     are done.

STEP 9. The rest of operations  [Tab 2, CCO, then quick role switches]  (2 minutes)
   - "Third parties": automatic tiering and review schedule.
   - "ESG": metrics with targets and evidence.
   - Optional: "Vetting" (as HR) and "Access reviews" (as CISO), one sentence each.

STEP 10. Who sees what  [Tab 1, switch accounts]  (2 minutes)
   - Retail BU manager (retail.manager@): a much shorter menu, and only Retail Banking
     alerts, controls, self-assessments and incidents.
     On "Self-assessments", click "Assess" on C-003 ("SAR submission within
     regulatory timeframe"), choose "Effective" and click "Submit assessment". Testing rated it only partially effective, so it is
     flagged straight away under "Disagree with tests".
     Say: "We ask managers, but we check their answers against the evidence."
   - Admin (admin@): "Cases and screening", Customers tab: names are masked.
   - Board (board@): read-only, no action buttons.
   - Say: "Access is checked on every request, not just hidden on screen."

STEP 11. Board report and audit trail  [Tab 1, CRO]  (2 minutes)
   - "Board report": written from live figures. Click "Print / save PDF".
   - "Audit log": click "Verify integrity". Point out that the actions you took during

STEP 12. Close  (1 minute)
   - "Integrations": the systems it connects to.
   - "Implementation": six phases over 26 weeks with sign-off gates.
   - Final line: "Real-time, evidence-based assurance, from the first alert to the
     board pack."

B4. QUESTIONS YOU WILL GET, AND HONEST ANSWERS
----------------------------------------------
   Q: Is this live bank data?
   A: No. It is realistic demo data. In production it reads from your core banking,
      CRM, HR and identity systems (the Integrations page shows them as "simulated").

   Q: Why UK regulators (FCA, PRA, NCA, ICO) and pounds?
   A: The demo is configured for a UK bank. Regulators, reports, notification
      deadlines and currency are configuration, and can be set for another country,
      for example CBN, NFIU, NDPC and naira for Nigeria.

   Q: Does it send emails or text messages?
   A: In-app notifications work in the demo. Email and SMS come with production.

   Q: Single sign-on and two-step verification?
   A: Planned for production (SAML or OIDC, plus authenticator-app codes). The demo
      only checks the demo password.

   Q: Can we change the rules and thresholds?
   A: Yes, from "Configure" on Control monitoring, with every change recorded in the
      audit log with a reason. New rules can be added.

   Q: Is the AML score AI?
   A: It is a transparent weighted model on purpose, so analysts and regulators can see
      why a case scored what it did. A machine-learning model can sit behind the same
      screen later.

   Q: Where is the data stored?
   A: In this demo, only in your browser. In production, in your own database, with
      evidence files in tamper-proof storage.


B5. PRACTICAL TIPS
------------------
   - If something goes wrong in practice, click "Reset demo data": back to the start
     in a second.
   - Changes are kept in the browser you used. Another computer starts clean.
   - Uploaded files can be downloaded only in the tab that uploaded them.
   - Sign in the second tab before step 4 so the four-eyes moment flows without typing.
   - Keep the board report open in a spare tab in case you need to jump to the end.


B6. WHAT THE DEMO DOES NOT DO (be upfront if asked)
---------------------------------------------------
   - No real data connections: a simulator stands in for them.
   - "Run now" on Control monitoring records a run but does not re-check new data.
   - No email or SMS, no single sign-on or two-step verification.
   - Data lives in the browser and resets when browser data is cleared.
